Shearlet systems

All three systems compute the same kind of transform: every subband is a periodic convolution of the input with a filter, so the transform is invariant under circular shifts and each subband keeps the size of the input. On the DFT grid each filter is a real, point-symmetric mask, so a transform is one FFT, then per subband a masked product and an inverse FFT. They differ in how the masks are built and in what frame they form.

Indexing

shearlet_layout(system, size(x)) lists the subbands in the order the coefficients stack them. A ShearletIndex has a scale (0 for the lowpass, then coarse to fine), a cone (the axis the subband's frequencies are largest along) and a shear per remaining axis. All systems share the shear convention: subband (cone c, shear k) collects the frequencies with $\omega_e / \omega_c \approx -k_e / s$, where $s$ is the number of shears per side of the cone at that scale.

Band-limited shearlets (FFST)

BandlimitedShearlets follows S. Häuser and G. Steidl, Fast Finite Shearlet Transform: a tutorial, arXiv:1202.1773. The shearlet of scale $j$, cone $c$ and shear $k$ is

\[\hat\psi_{j,c,k}(\omega) = \hat\psi_1\!\left(4^{1-j}\lVert\omega\rVert_\infty\right) \prod_{e \ne c} \hat\psi_2\!\left(2^{j-1}\frac{\omega_e}{\omega_c} + k_e\right),\]

with Meyer's radial window $\hat\psi_1$ and angular bump $\hat\psi_2$. The windows of a scale and the shears of a cone are partitions of unity, so with the seam shearlets glued across the cones the system is a Parseval frame: the inverse is the adjoint, and $\sum \lVert c_i\rVert^2 = \lVert x\rVert^2$.

  • smooth = true replaces the radial windows by the tensor-product construction of K. Guo and D. Labate (The construction of smooth Parseval frames of shearlets, MMNP 8(1), 2013), which makes the seam shearlets smooth.
  • alpha gives α-shearlets (F. Voigtlaender and A. Pein, arXiv:1702.03559): $2^{(2-\alpha)(j-1)}$ shears per side at scale $j$.
  • On 3-D data the cones are the three pyramids around the axes; a seam shearlet is glued across every pyramid whose element has a parallel central direction.

MATLAB's shearletSystem is built after the same tutorial but on its own grid, with the seam halves kept apart and a final normalisation, so it is not a bitwise reference for this implementation.

Nonsubsampled shearlet transform (NSST)

NSSTShearlets is G. Easley, D. Labate and W.-Q. Lim, Sparse directional image representations using the discrete shearlet transform, ACHA 25(1), 2008: a nonsubsampled Laplacian pyramid (MultiscaleFilterBanks' à trous pyramid, the one the NSCT uses) followed by shearing filters on every level. The shearing filters are Meyer windows on the pseudo-polar slope, sampled on an $L \times L$ grid and applied as $L \times L$ spatial filters; they sum to one, so the inverse adds the directional subbands of a level and inverts the pyramid.

Compactly supported shearlets (ShearLab 3D)

CompactShearlets is G. Kutyniok, W.-Q. Lim and R. Reisenhofer, ShearLab 3D: Faithful digital shearlet transforms based on compactly supported shearlets, ACM TOMS 42(1), 2016, with the nonseparable generator of W.-Q. Lim, Nonseparable shearlet transform, IEEE TIP 22(5), 2013: a 1-D wavelet across the cone, a scaling function along it and a 2-D fan filter, digitally sheared on a refined grid. The filters are compactly supported in space, and the frame is not tight; the inverse uses the dual filters $\hat\psi / \sum|\hat\psi|^2$.

The quadrature mirror filter is ShearLab's 9-tap maximally flat lowpass; the fan filter is the maximally flat fan of order 4 of A. L. da Cunha, J. Zhou and M. N. Do (IEEE TIP 15(10), 2006, Table II). The construction follows Definition 3.2 of the ShearLab 3D paper; on 3-D data, Definitions 3.3–3.4.
